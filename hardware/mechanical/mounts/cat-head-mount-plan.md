# Cat Head Mount Plan

## Purpose

Plan a front-mounted cat head or mask that gives the bike its Bio-Luminescent Abyssinian identity without compromising ride safety or serviceability.

## Design Goals

- Faceted copper or rose-gold cat face.
- Illuminated cyan/aqua eyes.
- Internal glow through selected face facets.
- Fiber-optic whiskers exiting both sides, lit by hidden LEDs inside the head.
- Removable mount for transport and repair.

## Candidate Mount Locations

| Location | Pros | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| Handlebar center / stem area | Easy access, visible, rotates naturally with steering | Can interfere with cables and rider controls if too large or poorly placed. |
| Head tube/front frame | More stable visually | Needs custom bracket and steering-clearance checks. |
| Front rack/headlight area | Good forward identity | Must not block existing headlight or front brake cable. |

Current direction:

- First version should rotate with the handlebars.
- Preferred mount point is the handlebar center / stem area or a custom bracket tied into that area.
- Avoid adding a front rack unless the cat head design specifically needs a hidden structural support.

## Initial Size Envelope

Use this as the starting point for the cardboard/foamcore mockup. Final dimensions should be adjusted after measuring and steering-clearance tests.

| Dimension | Initial Target | Tentative Range | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Rigid head width | 14 in | 12-16 in | Keep rigid head inside handlebar width. |
| Rigid head height | 12 in | 10-14 in | Must preserve rider sightline and headlight visibility. |
| Forward depth / projection | 8 in | 6-10 in | Too much forward mass may affect steering feel. |
| Flexible whisker length | 10 in each side | 8-12 in each side | Flexible, non-pokey, and away from hands/cables. |
| Weight | Under 2 lb | Ideally near 1 lb | Keep steering light and mount loads reasonable. |

Rigid sculpture should stay inside the handlebar width. Flexible whiskers may extend wider if they are soft, rounded, and safe.

## Clearance Requirements

- Full left/right steering without cable pull or sculpture contact.
- No interference with brake levers, shifter, throttle/display, or grips.
- No obstruction of the rider's forward sightline.
- Existing headlight remains visible or is intentionally replaced.
- Whiskers cannot touch hands, brake levers, cables, tire, or pedestrians during normal use.

## Mount and Envelope Test Plan

1. Build a rough cardboard/foamcore head at the initial target size: 14 in wide, 12 in high, 8 in deep.
2. Add temporary 10 in whisker placeholders on each side.
3. Temporarily attach the mockup near the handlebar center / stem area.
4. Turn handlebars fully left and right.
5. Check brake cables, display/throttle/control access, grips, brake levers, and headlight beam.
6. Sit on the bike and check rider sightline.
7. Walk around the bike and check whether the head reads clearly from front and side.
8. Adjust scale before adding final finish, LEDs, or fiber optics.

## Fabrication Options

- Lightweight plastic mask with metallic film or paint.
- Laser-cut or hand-cut faceted panels over a lightweight frame.
- 3D printed frame with translucent diffuser panels.
- Modified ready-made cat mask with added lighting and mount points.

## Material and Finish Options

Separate the head into two concerns:

- Structure: the lightweight shape that mounts safely to the bike.
- Surface finish: the copper/rose-gold/mirror/faceted visual layer.

| Option | Use | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cardboard / foamcore origami shell | First mockup | Fast, cheap, easy to cut/fold, good for size and steering clearance tests | Not playa durable unless heavily sealed; weak in moisture | Strongly recommended for first physical mockup |
| Ready-made cat mask | Fast MVP head base | Existing face shape, quick to modify | May look costume-like; may need reinforcement; less faceted | Good fallback or quick prototype |
| 3D printed frame/shell | Custom final or semi-final structure | Custom mounting bosses, LED holes, fiber routing, repeatable shape | Print time, finishing work, size limits | Good final direction if fabrication time allows |
| Laser-cut acrylic/PETG/polycarbonate facets | Final faceted shell | Clean flat facets, works well with mirror film and translucent lit panels | Requires design/fab effort; edges need finishing | Best polished final direction |
| Thin plywood | Shape prototype or internal frame | Easy to cut, strong enough for tests | Needs sealing; less sleek; can be heavier | Useful for mockups/brackets |
| Sheet metal | Decorative accent only | Real metal look | Heavy, sharp, harder to fabricate, less crash-friendly | Avoid as main structure |

## Finish Options

| Finish | Look | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper / rose-gold mirror vinyl | True reflective faceted look | Lightweight, good on flat facets, replaceable, practical | Wrinkles on compound curves; edges need care | Preferred for faceted panels |
| Mirrored acrylic / mirrored PETG | Very polished mirror facets | Durable, reflective, laser-cuttable | More expensive and more fabrication work | Strong final option |
| Mylar / mirror craft film | Cheap reflective prototype | Fast, very shiny | Wrinkles easily, not durable | Good for mockup only |
| Metallic copper/rose-gold paint | Metallic but not true mirror | Easy on complex shapes, good for edges/internal structure | Usually not truly mirror-like; clear coat can dull it | Good secondary finish, not primary mirror strategy |
| Real metal panels | Real copper/metal look | Authentic material | Heavy, sharp edges, vibration noise, harder mounting | Avoid except small accents |

Paint alone is unlikely to deliver a true mirror look. The preferred mirror strategy is flat faceted geometry plus copper/rose-gold mirror vinyl or mirrored plastic.

## Iteration Plan

### Iteration 1: Paper/Cardboard/ Foamcore Envelope

- Use the online origami/faceted cat instructions as a starting point.
- Build a rough full-scale mockup.
- Test size, steering clearance, rider sightline, brake cable clearance, and headlight visibility.
- No final LEDs required.
- Optional: apply cheap Mylar or mirror film to a few facets to judge the look.

### Iteration 2: Daytime Visual Mockup

- Refine the shell size and facet layout.
- Apply copper/rose-gold mirror vinyl to opaque facets.
- Mark candidate translucent eye/facet locations.
- Add temporary whisker placeholders using fiber, wire, or plastic rods.
- Test how it looks on the bike in daylight.

### Iteration 3: Lit Prototype

- Add cyan/aqua eye lighting.
- Add hidden LED light engines for side-glow fiber whiskers.
- Add a few internal facet glow zones.
- Add a temporary quick disconnect.
- Bench test brightness and heat before riding.

### Iteration 4: Rideable Module

- Replace fragile mockup materials with plastic, printed frame, acrylic/PETG facets, or reinforced shell.
- Add final mount, safety tether, strain relief, and sealed cable exits.
- Verify full steering/brake/headlight/rider clearance.
- Make it removable for transport and post-Burning-Man bike use.

## Mounting Hardware Ideas

- Clamp-on bracket to handlebar or stem.
- U-bolts with rubber isolation.
- Quick-release fasteners for transport.
- Secondary safety tether to frame.
